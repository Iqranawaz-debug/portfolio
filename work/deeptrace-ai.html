DeepTrace AI

A local-first research workspace that has to show you the sentence before it’s allowed to make the claim — and a code check that throws out citations that don’t hold up.

01

The problem

AI research assistants are fast, but hard to trust.

  • They cite sources that don’t contain the quoted words.
  • They mix opinion with evidence, and rarely say what they couldn’t find.
  • They usually send your questions and documents off your machine.
02

Why it matters

A wrong answer with a confident citation is worse than no answer: it borrows the credibility of a source that never said it.

If the citation is the thing people check, the citation is the thing the system has to get right — every time, provably, without asking a second model to vouch for the first.

03

My contribution

Everything, solo, in phases: I wrote the product requirements and UI design, recorded the architecture decisions, then built and tested the foundation, the investigation workspace, evidence collection, the Milestone 1 research pipeline and the final workspace redesign.

I closed the project deliberately on 9 November 2026 with a handoff that lists what works, what was verified, what is broken and how to pick it up again.

04

Architecture & decisions

One local FastAPI process, one SQLite database and a React workspace. A run moves through five bounded stages; hitting any limit stops the run as “Budget reached” and keeps what it found.

  1. PlanHard limits on searches, pages, steps, tokens and minutes
  2. DiscoverTavily search, within the search limit
  3. ReadPolicy-checked fetches; robots.txt; ≤4 passages per source
  4. CheckGemini drafts from ≤12 passages; code validates each citation
  5. ReportImmutable, SHA-256-checked Markdown, partial runs labelled
  • Citations are checked by code, not by a model.A finding is “Cited” only if its passage exists in this investigation, was among the passages the model was allowed to use, and contains any quotation the model presented. Otherwise it is “Not established”; failing citations are suppressed and counted.
  • The model never chooses a URL.It has no tools. Requests to private or internal addresses are refused, connections are pinned to the address that passed the checks, and redirects and proxies are never followed.
  • Local and private by default.Mock mode is the default. Live mode needs local configuration, your own keys and consent per provider. Keys live in Windows Credential Manager, never in the database, and a “sensitive” flag keeps an investigation away from hosted models entirely.
  • Fail honestly.When a run fails it says why, and partial reports are labelled as partial rather than dressed up as complete.

06

Limitations & next steps

The big one: citation validation proves the reference exists and the quotation matches. It does not prove the passage supports the claim. The interface says so wherever the checks are shown.

  • Claim-support assessment (Milestone 3), PDFs and local semantic search (Milestone 2), and packaging (Milestone 4) were planned and not built.
  • The Gemini free tier is often busy and no fallback model is configured; a failed run keeps its sources and explains the failure.
  • Evidence can lean on a few sources: diversity caps help, but nothing judges source credibility.
  • Windows only: credentials, CI and browser tests assume Windows and Microsoft Edge.
  • Not measured: frontend coverage, a manual screen-reader audit, a penetration test or a CVE scan.

Recommended next steps from the handoff: confirm CI on GitHub, add a fallback when the model is busy, then build independent claim-support checks shown separately from the deterministic ones.

07

Outcome

A working, tested Milestone 1: a bounded research pipeline that ran end to end on real services and caught its own bad citations on the first try.

The lasting lesson is about scope: deciding what a check can honestly claim — and printing that limit next to the result — mattered as much as the pipeline itself.
